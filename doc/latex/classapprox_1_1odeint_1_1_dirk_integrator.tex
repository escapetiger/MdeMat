\doxysubsection{Detailed Description}
Diagonally Implicit Runge-\/\+Kutta integrator. 

\doxylink{classapprox_1_1odeint_1_1_dirk_integrator}{Dirk\+Integrator} implements Diagonally Implicit Runge-\/\+Kutta (DIRK) methods for solving ordinary differential equations of the form\+:

\[\frac{\mathrm{d}(Mu)}{\mathrm{d}t} = Lu + S,
\]

where $M$ is a mass operator, $L$ is a linear operator, and $S$ is a source term. Any of these terms can be empty. For mass operator, it means it reduces to an identity. For linear and source term, it means they vanish from the equation.

DIRK methods are characterized by a lower triangular Butcher tableau with identical diagonal elements, allowing each stage to be solved independently. This makes them more efficient than fully implicit Runge-\/\+Kutta methods while maintaining good stability properties.

Compute stage i of the augmented DIRK method. 

U = stage\+A(obj, i, L, S, M, P, A, B, C) computes the solution at stage i by solving the augmented implicit system with constraints. The method handles both differential and algebraic variables simultaneously\+:

\[\begin{bmatrix}
M - \Delta t \cdot A_{ii} L & -\Delta t \cdot A_{ii}
P \\ -A & -B
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} u^{(i)} \\ v^{(i)} \end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} \text{rhs}_u \\ C \end{bmatrix}
\]

where $u^{(i)}$ are the differential variables, $v^{(i)}$ are the algebraic variables, and the right-\/hand side includes contributions from the mass operator, previous stages, and source terms. Advance one time step using the augmented DIRK method. 

U = step\+A(obj, L, S, M, P, A, B, C) performs a complete time step for the constrained system by computing all augmented stages and combining them according to the Butcher tableau weights. The method solves the differential-\/algebraic equation system\+:

\[\frac{\mathrm{d}(Mu)}{\mathrm{d}t} = Lu + Pv + S, \quad
0 = Au + Bv + C
\]

where {\itshape u} represents the differential variables, {\itshape v} represents the algebraic variables, and the constraint equation couples them through operators {\itshape A} and {\itshape B}. Each stage is solved implicitly using the diagonal structure of the DIRK method.
